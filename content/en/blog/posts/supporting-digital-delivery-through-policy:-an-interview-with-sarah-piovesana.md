<p class="has-background" style="background-color:#ebf2fa">As part of a new ongoing Q&amp;A series spotlighting our employees, CDS introduces you to dedicated individuals whose outstanding work contributes to the success of our mission.<br>Below is the interview held with <a href="http://linkedin.com/in/sarah-piovesana-9ab52baa" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Sarah Piovesana (Senior Policy Advisor)</a>.</p>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>How would you describe your team’s function and your role within it?</strong></h2>



<p>“When you build a software product in the Government of Canada, there are a number of laws and policies that apply to you as a team in delivering that work. Policy Advisors evaluate the key features and functionality, product vision, user stories, or jobs-to-be-done and identify policy questions that, when answered, enable the team to move forward with intention and clarity that whatever we’re doing reflects the values, objectives, and requirements behind those laws and policies. We learn about the connected systems, technical decisions, design interventions, and data flows to provide specific and actionable policy advice.”&nbsp;</p>



<p>“We embed Policy Advisors directly on product teams to help people, such as product managers, designers, design researchers, data scientists and engineers, and developers who may not have experience (or capacity in) navigating those laws and policies. We highlight specific requirements and complete the compliance processes. I have been embedded on several product teams, <a href="https://articles.alpha.canada.ca/forms-formulaires/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">GC Forms</a>, <a href="https://design-system.canada.ca/en/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">GC Design System</a>, GC Articles, <a href="https://www.canada.ca/en/government/system/digital-government/digital-government-innovations/digital-credentials.html#:~:text=identity%20multiple%20times.-,GC%20Issue%20and%20Verify,-The%20GC%20Issue" target="_blank" rel="noopener">GC Issue and Verify</a>, and most recently <a href="https://login.canada.ca/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">CanadaLogin</a>.”</p>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>How does working alongside designers, developers, design researchers, and product managers change the way policy advice is delivered?&nbsp;</strong></h2>



<p>“Traditionally, policy advice is captured through policy position papers, briefing notes, and may be formalized through Cabinet processes, such as a Memorandum to Cabinet or a Treasury Board submission.&nbsp;</p>



<p>Product team members don’t need you to write a briefing note for them. They typically need one page that explains:</p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li>What is the requirement?</li>



<li>How are we interpreting it?</li>



<li>What does it mean for them?</li>



<li>What do they need to do next?”&nbsp;</li>
</ul>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>What is an ongoing challenge for the Delivery Policy team?&nbsp;</strong></h2>



<p>“We’re always thinking about legitimacy and authority: asking ourselves, ‘Do we have any business saying what we’re saying and doing what we’re doing?’. The policy function lends itself to answering that question: ‘Are we building tools that reflect the Government of Canada’s priorities, values, and policy frameworks? How can we do things differently while honouring those considerations?’ That’s the overarching challenge for us. That’s why our team was created.”&nbsp;</p>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>What skills or qualities do you think make someone successful in delivering policy?</strong></h2>



<p>“When someone says, ‘We heard that we can’t do that,’ we always have follow-up questions: ‘Who said that to you? What did they base it off of? What is actually behind that?’ You have to have the judgement and experience to say, ‘I don’t think that’s true’ and go and actually find out for yourself.”&nbsp;</p>



<p class="has-background" style="background-color:#ebf2fa"><strong>“A lot of curiosity is important; you need to be able to bring together many perspectives and work through them with the team.”</strong></p>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>What would you say is your favourite part of working on the Delivery Policy team?</strong></h2>



<p>“If you consider the user journey of a person signing up for and signing in with their <a href="https://digital.canada.ca/2026/09/28/simplifying-access-to-government-programs-and-services-with-canadalogin/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">CanadaLogin</a>, everything about that was influenced by a multidisciplinary team coming together to shape it. My favourite part of delivery policy is being a part of that process. Working with our designer to review every user interaction and task, considering the right moments to share important information to meet the legal and policy requirements given what we know about how people use digital services and any technical considerations about the software functionality. This helped ensure that the first release of <a href="https://digital.canada.ca/2026/09/28/simplifying-access-to-government-programs-and-services-with-canadalogin/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">CanadaLogin</a> took a balanced approach between meeting requirements without overloading users. Alongside the privacy notice statement and terms and conditions, we shared supplementary information throughout the user journey so people could understand what is happening with their personal information.”&nbsp;</p>
