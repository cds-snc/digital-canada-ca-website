<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Seven departments transitioned from legacy technology to CanadaLogin.</strong></h3>



<p>If you use PrairiesCan, Financial Consumer Agency of Canada Secure Portal, Veterans Affairs Canada Healthshare, Canada Economic Development for Quebec Regions Client Space, MyCGC (Canadian Grain Commission), Digital Talent, or either of the two Access To Information and Privacy portals, you may have noticed something new. That’s CanadaLogin, the government’s new way to sign in to government online programs and services.</p>



<p>Here’s a look at what changed and why.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>What is CanadaLogin</strong></h3>



<p>In 2024, the Government of Canada invested in one modern sign in service for federal online programs and services and allocated funding to Canadian Digital Service (CDS) to build a single sign on solution, CanadaLogin.&nbsp;</p>



<p>Over the past two years, CDS has <a href="https://digital.canada.ca/2026/09/28/simplifying-access-to-government-programs-and-services-with-canadalogin/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">developed CanadaLogin</a>, designed to be easy to use, secure, and scalable. The goal is simple: to help people access government programs and services safely and without hassle. </p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>The legacy technology</strong></h3>



<p>Sign In Canada (SIC)</p>



<p>The idea for a single front door to Government of Canada programs and services goes back years, and was included strategic plans like the <a href="https://www.tbs-sct.canada.ca/pol/doc-eng.aspx?id=32603" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Policy on Service and Digital Development</a> and the <a href="https://publications.gc.ca/site/archivee-archived.html?url=https%3A%2F%2Fpublications.gc.ca%2Fcollections%2Fcollection_2019%2Fsct-tbs%2FBT39-46-2018-eng.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Digital Operations Strategic Plan</a> (now the <a href="https://www.canada.ca/en/government/system/digital-government/canada-digital-ambition.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Digital Ambition</a>). In 2018-2019, SIC was created as a proof of concept intended to modernize and eventually replace the existing Government of Canada authentication services, including GCKey. The objective was to modernize the Government of Canada authentication ecosystem into a platform that would provide a single point of entry for people and businesses accessing online Government of Canada programs and services.</p>



<p>SIC was created to demonstrate that:</p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li>A common sign-in experience could work across government.</li>



<li>Single Sign-On (SSO) and Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA) could be offered through a shared service.</li>



<li>Departments could avoid building and maintaining their own authentication solutions.</li>
</ul>



<p>And that is exactly what it did.</p>



<p>Between 2018 and 2023, SIC was a pilot for the Government of Canada&#8217;s future digital authentication ecosystem. In 2023, it was <a href="https://connect.canada.ca/en/announcement.html#july-31st-2023" target="_blank" rel="noopener">put into maintenance mode</a> because work was underway to procure and implement an enterprise-wide Government of Canada Customer Identity and Access Management (CIAM) solution. SIC’s decommissioning, targeted for November 2026, marks the successful completion of an important chapter in digital modernization and the beginning of the next phase of enterprise authentication services.&nbsp;</p>



<p>The work of a product continues beyond decommissioning, including knowledge transfer, transitioning and/or offboarding users, documenting, and storytelling. The remnants of a decommissioned product and the expertise the team developed along the way can be the kernel of the next big thing. You can learn more about the life cycle of a product in our <a href="https://digital.canada.ca/2024/11/05/measuring-progress-a-product-maturity-model-for-digital-government/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">product maturity model</a>.</p>



<p><strong>Decommissioning is not the end, but the continuation of the important work being done to bring people and businesses better access to Government of Canada programs and services.</strong></p>



<p>Over time, CanadaLogin will replace other legacy credentials, like GCKey, in an effort to create a single, modern sign-in experience when accessing Government of Canada programs and services and improve the overall sign in experience.</p>



<p><strong>How seven departments made the switch</strong></p>



<p>We decided to start with departments (or “partners”) using SIC because they represented a smaller group of lower-volume services, allowing us to test, evaluate, and refine the migration process before scaling it more broadly. They were also dedicated enough to the cause of improving the sign in experience to try something new and learn along with us. This approach helps us better support additional departments and agencies as they transition to CanadaLogin in the future.&nbsp;</p>



<p>Moving a department to a new sign in platform takes careful planning and teamwork. From September 2025 to April 2026, we helped prepare our SIC partners for their transition to CanadaLogin.</p>



<p>In April this year, the first SIC partner launched with CanadaLogin, and by September 2026, all seven SIC partners had officially transitioned to CanadaLogin.</p>



<p>This new system marks an important milestone in the modernization of government digital services. SIC is the first platform to be replaced by CanadaLogin.</p>



<p>Behind this achievement lies hard work, collaboration, and innovation from our teams and partners.</p>



<p>Explore the key milestones in the evolution of authentication services that led to the creation of CanadaLogin:</p>



<p><strong>2016-2017</strong></p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li>Need to evolve cyber authentication formally recognized and mentioned in strategic plans (<a href="https://www.tbs-sct.canada.ca/pol/doc-eng.aspx?id=32603" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Policy on Service and Digital Development</a> and the <a href="https://publications.gc.ca/site/archivee-archived.html?url=https%3A%2F%2Fpublications.gc.ca%2Fcollections%2Fcollection_2019%2Fsct-tbs%2FBT39-46-2018-eng.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Digital Operations Strategic Plan</a>)</li>
</ul>



<p><strong>2018</strong></p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li>Decision made to develop the Sign In Canada (SIC) proof of concept in-house</li>
</ul>



<p><strong>2019</strong></p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li>Interim Authority to Operate (ATO) received to promote SIC</li>
</ul>



<p><strong>2021</strong></p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li>First production launch of SIC with PacifiCan/PrairiesCan.</li>
</ul>



<p><strong>2022</strong></p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li>SIC expanded across multiple departments and programs.</li>
</ul>



<p><strong>2023-2024</strong></p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li>Budget 2024 allocated funding for CDS to build a SSO solution, now called CanadaLogin</li>



<li>Joint Deputy Minister decision was made to transfer public-facing legacy authentication services from Shared Services Canada (SSC) to CDS to align with the Government’s digital modernization agenda</li>



<li>CIAM SaaS was procured by December 2024</li>
</ul>



<p><strong>2025</strong></p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li>Accountability for legacy authentication services formally transferred from SSC to CDS, solidifying CDS’s role as the leader of GC’s enterprise digital credential initiatives</li>



<li>Interim Authority to Operate (ATO) received</li>
</ul>



<p><strong>2026</strong></p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li>All 7 SIC partners transitioned over to CanadaLogin</li>



<li><a href="https://digital.canada.ca/2026/09/28/simplifying-access-to-government-programs-and-services-with-canadalogin/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">CanadaLogin officially launches</a></li>



<li>Sign In Canada (SIC) decommissioned November 2026</li>
</ul>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>A collaborative effort</strong></h3>



<p>None of this work would have been possible without numerous teams and departments coming together to work towards a common goal. This was a whole-of-government effort; executive and technical experts, developers, human resources specialists, communications experts, usability testers, project managers, consultants, and so many more worked tirelessly to get CanadaLogin off the ground. Feedback from our partners was instrumental in improving CanadaLogin and enhancing the onboarding experience for new partners. Thank you to everyone involved.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>What’s next</strong></h3>



<p>We have big plans for the future of digital government, and we hope to reach them one step at a time. Here are what the next few steps will look like:</p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li>Onboarding more partners across the GC, scaling to include critical programs and services with larger user bases, like benefits, taxes, and immigration</li>



<li>Identity proofing capabilities including online real-time options and in-person options.</li>



<li>Greater interoperability with trusted digital identities from provinces and territories</li>
</ul>



<p>We are very excited about CanadaLogin, and the potential it has to revolutionise access to Government of Canada programs and services through a common authentication experience.</p>
